Translation plugin or real Chinese pages?

17 November 2026·UX CIRCLES

Many firms that serve Chinese-speaking clients add a translation button to their website. It is quick and cheap, and it looks like the problem is solved. But a client who reads the Chinese version often gets a very different impression from the one you intended.

What a translation plugin gets wrong

  • Legal and financial terms come out wrong. Machine translation picks a common meaning, not the one used in your field. A term like "conveyancing" or "indefinite leave to remain" can come out as something no Hong Kong or Taiwanese client would recognise.
  • It translates things it shouldn't. Names, addresses, account IDs and form labels can all get translated. I have seen a WeChat ID turned into words in another language, so anyone who copied it couldn't find the firm.
  • Code shows up on the page. When a plugin is set up incorrectly, visitors can see raw code such as a shortcode in square brackets at the top of the page. It looks like a broken website.
  • It may not help clients find you. Some plugins only translate the page in the visitor's browser, so there is no Chinese page for search engines to find. A client searching in Chinese may never see you.
  • It doesn't sound like you. Clients choosing a solicitor or accountant are looking for someone they can trust. Stiff, machine-made Chinese suggests the firm doesn't really work in Chinese, even when it does.

When real Chinese pages are worth it

You don't need to translate your whole website. Start with the pages that decide whether a client contacts you:

  1. Homepage: who you are, who you help, and how to contact you.
  2. Your main services: for example, BN(O) visas, property, or tax returns.
  3. Fees: clear prices in Chinese build more trust than anything else.
  4. Contact: with WhatsApp, phone and address, written correctly.

Write these in Chinese, not just translate them. Use the words your clients use. A Hong Kong client says 按揭 for mortgage; a Taiwanese client says 房貸. If your team speaks Chinese, ask them to read every page before it goes live.

A five-minute check

  • Switch your website to Chinese. Would you be comfortable sending this page to a client?
  • Are names, phone numbers and IDs still correct in the Chinese version?
  • Is any code or English placeholder text visible?
  • Search for your main service in Chinese. Does your website appear?
  • Does the Chinese version have a clear way to contact you?
